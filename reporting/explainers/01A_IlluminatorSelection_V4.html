Report 01A · historical design analysis

How the team chose HDTV illuminator candidates and observation geometry

A human-readable account of the original MATLAB link-budget work: what was compared, what the models produced, what changed next, and what still needs field evidence.

Executive answer: Two different decisions came from the historical work. The target-region model favored Hudson over Newton (69.91 dB versus 57.78 dB mean modeled SNR), while the first practical installation prototype used Apple Hill East Garage because it was accessible for adjustment and had a Newton/Needham direction in the site comparison. The historical model preference did not become a verified installed-transmitter selection. Both findings are design history—not current field qualification or aircraft-detection evidence.

2. The engineering decision chain

In simple terms: first identify broadcasts that are geographically plausible; then see whether a roof can obtain a clean enough copy of the broadcast; then ask whether the same transmitter–receiver geometry can illuminate the airspace of interest; then carry the promising cases into RF design.

3. What information went into the analysis?

Broadcast and tower inputs

Location, antenna height, directional behavior, channel/frequency, bandwidth, and transmitter-power/ERP records were gathered for candidate sites. The original source contains both station-derived ERP records and later equal 500 kW placeholder inputs; they are not silently merged. SRC-01 · SRC-03

Receiver-site inputs

Apple Hill East Garage, Apple Hill West Garage, and LS Garage were compared for line-of-sight windows, aircraft view, power, Wi-Fi, proximity, and the ability to adjust the prototype. SRC-04

Airspace and propagation inputs

The model placed target points in a drawn region of interest and used terrain-aware propagation. The later Live Script used 6,000 ft and 0.5 m² bistatic RCS; an earlier deck slide used a 3,000 ft ROI label. SRC-01 · SRC-04 · SRC-06

Hudson tower record: 42.384083°, −71.493139°, 400 kW station-record ERP calculation; the record also notes 100 kW vertical ERP. These are historical station records, not the Live Script's model inputs. SRC-03
Values that changed or remain unresolvedThe later Live Script explicitly uses 500 kW placeholder ERP for both HDTV cases. Its wide-area receiver input is 25 m, while the local building ray-tracing script sets the receiver to 0 m to match the garage height; these are different historical modeling contexts, not one reconciled site value. The earlier deck labels its direct-path screen as FM-powered. The project has not used any of these historical items to establish current transmitter verification, installed-path adequacy, or collection suitability. SRC-01 · SRC-02 · SRC-04 · SRC-09

Candidate-tower source provenance

The candidate survey began with a saved RabbitEars Boston market-detail HTML page. The original scraper.ipynb describes extracting station-level location, height, ERP, channel, and RF-program information from that page. The LinkBudget work then used its own checked-in Radio_Tower_List.txt as the direct MATLAB input for tower coordinates, heights, powers, gains, and later Newton/Hudson screening.

What this establishesThe candidate records were assembled from an initial local broadcast survey and then used as an input to the MATLAB geometry analysis. The source material identifies the saved survey and parser; it does not preserve a named provider, a Leif Hille handoff, a FlightTestAE transfer record, or a fully reproducible transformation from the notebook outputs to the LinkBudget copy.
Source-discovery boundaryThe saved market page identifies RabbitEars and includes a note that its network data comes from the FCC but is a quick reference whose accuracy is not guaranteed. Regional screen — Which broad tower-to-rooftop directions appeared plausible?

Why direct path matters

A passive receiver needs a usable copy of the broadcast as a comparison/reference waveform. If terrain or nearby structures block the tower direction, later processing begins with an avoidable disadvantage.

What the site comparison showed

Apple Hill East Garage had clear LOS toward Needham/Newton and an obstruction toward Hudson/Marlborough. Apple Hill West Garage showed the opposite broad pattern. LS Garage had relatively clear windows to both but retained tree and tower-height caveats. SRC-04, slide 14

What followed

The source explicitly documents Apple Hill East Garage as the prototype site because it was near the office and easy to revisit while adjusting. It also says LS Garage might be used for full collection later. SRC-04, slide 15

Local building screen — the regional terrain map asks which broad directions are plausible; the original Apple Hill building study asks whether nearby building geometry could alter the path close to the rooftop.

1B

Could nearby Apple Hill buildings affect the close-to-receiver path?

Question

Could nearby Apple Hill buildings affect the direct path close to the receiver?

Why we tested it

A broad terrain line-of-sight screen can miss a close building obstruction near the rooftop.

Method

The Live Script reads applehill.osm, extracts buildings in an area of interest, imports them into Site Viewer, then configures SBR ray tracing with up to three reflections and two diffractions. It sets the local receiver height to 0 m to match the garage-height study. SRC-02

Evidence

Figure 1B is the original Apple Hill building-area output embedded in the Live Script package. It is retained as a stable report asset without redrawing or recompressing it. FIG-08 · SRC-12

Result

The recovered source output visualizes the actual building geometry selected for local analysis. It does not retain a source-faithful 3D Site Viewer or ray-path rendering, so no quantitative local propagation result is claimed.

Decision

Use the local-building study as supporting context for the rooftop comparison and Apple Hill East Garage prototype investigation, not as the sole basis for that practical choice.

Remaining uncertainty

Modeled local propagation is not measured received power, measured multipath, installed-antenna performance, a qualified Reference Path, or field-collection acceptance.

Question

Why we tested it

Direct-path availability is a precondition for a future passive comparison. It also affects where an antenna can practically be mounted and serviced.

Method

The code uses geometric los checks, terrain-aware propagation, and a separate building-aware SBR ray-tracing setup at Apple Hill. The building script imports an Apple Hill OpenStreetMap building area and allows up to three reflections and two diffractions. SRC-01 · SRC-02

The source found different line-of-sight windows by garage: Apple Hill East was favorable toward Newton/Needham, while Apple Hill West was favorable toward Hudson/Marlborough. The result was a geometry and practicality screen, not a measured link test.

Decision

Geometric visibility and a ray trace are not measured received power, lock evidence, role verification, or reference-path adequacy. Those remain separate current gates. SRC-09 · SRC-10

2

Which geometry best illuminates the intended airspace?

Question

Among candidate transmitter–receiver geometries, which one produced a more useful modeled signal level across the intended aircraft region?

Why we tested it

A powerful transmitter is not automatically useful. The reflected path depends on where an aircraft is relative to both transmitter and receiver.

Method

The Live Script creates a target grid inside a drawn ROI, uses terrain elevation plus target altitude, calculates transmitter-to-target and target-to-receiver paths, and combines the assumed bistatic target properties with propagation loss. SRC-01 · SRC-05 · SRC-06

Evidence

The original ROI map and the paired SNR coverage figures below document the airspace model and the resulting historical comparison. FIG-03 · FIG-04 · FIG-05

Result

The original slide-42 comparison reports Hudson with the higher mean modeled field-of-view SNR: 69.91 dB, compared with 57.78 dB for Newton. It also reports modeled target powers of −67.3723 dBm and −79.5023 dBm, respectively. SRC-04, slide 42

Decision

Use the comparison to determine which cases were worth carrying forward to receiver-design analysis; do not use it to make a field-performance claim. Hudson was preferred in this historical modeled comparison. SRC-04

Remaining uncertainty

The model's ERP, altitude, RCS, terrain, integration, and propagation assumptions affect the result. A modeled SNR is neither a measured aircraft echo nor an aircraft-detection result.

3

Turn geometry results into receiver-design inputs

Question

Which candidate channel and predicted signal conditions were worth using as inputs to the next RF-budget analysis?

Why we tested it

The receive chain must be designed for a plausible direct-path signal and a much weaker target-path signal. Candidate channel frequency and bandwidth determine the hardware-design operating point.

Method

The historical Live Script entered Newton at 596.3 MHz and Hudson at 548 MHz, both with 6 MHz bandwidth; it calculated historical direct-path outputs and target-region quantities. Station records and site tradeoffs supplied contextual cross-checks. SRC-01 · SRC-03 · SRC-04

Evidence

Saved Live Script output contains historical modeled direct-path values; the original deck's paired comparison gives the visible region-level result. The candidate table below preserves the source discrepancy instead of selecting a convenient ERP value. SRC-01 · SRC-04

Result

The analysis made the frequency/bandwidth cases and their predicted levels concrete enough to become inputs to RF-chain reasoning. It did not establish the installed receiver's headroom, impedance behavior, reference quality, or clipping margin.

Decision

Use the investigated channel cases, historical modeled direct-path levels, target-region comparison, and unresolved ERP assumptions as the required starting inputs for 01B_ReceiveChainDesign. The sources inspected here do not document a final transferred RF-budget configuration. That next report owns filters, gain, impedance, and installed-chain qualification.

Remaining uncertainty

The Live Script's equal 500 kW ERP entries are explicitly placeholders, while the station file records different values. The historical design analysis does not verify the current broadcast configuration or a final field channel selection. SRC-01 · SRC-03

9. What the analysis did not prove

Do not promote a historical design model into current field evidence. The original work was valuable because it determined where to spend engineering effort. It did not close today’s collection or downstream-performance gates.
Direct-path model ≠ measured direct path

The historical dBm outputs are code/model outputs. They are not received-power measurements at the installed receiver.

FM screen ≠ DTV result

The deck itself says its direct-path screen used FM transmit power. Relative geometry was useful; DTV SNR was not final.

Line of sight ≠ reference qualification

A visible direction does not demonstrate antenna role, lock, dropped-sample integrity, reference adequacy, or collection suitability.

Modeled target SNR ≠ aircraft detection

It is not a live-aircraft echo, operational Pd/Pfa, tracking, or localization result.

Current-state boundary: Report 02 documents physical collection infrastructure, but the Reference Path and collection suitability remain blocked pending controlled field evidence. Report 06 keeps formal detection, tracking, and localization claims disabled. One modeled bistatic link is a future constrained measurement opportunity—not localization. SRC-09 · SRC-10

12. Glossary

Direct path

The broadcast signal traveling from the transmitter directly to the receiver. It is the candidate source for a comparison/reference waveform; it is not automatically a qualified Reference Path.

ERP

Effective radiated power: a way of describing the transmitter’s radiated strength in a particular direction. It can differ by polarization and antenna pattern, which is why an unverified placeholder is risky.

Region of interest (ROI)

The selected patch of airspace in which model target points were placed so that geometries could be compared across an area, not just along a line.

Bistatic RCS

A modeled measure of how strongly a target reflects energy from a transmitter toward a separate receiver. Here it is an assumption used for historical comparison, not an aircraft measurement.

SNR

Signal-to-noise ratio. In these figures it is a model output used to compare candidate geometry under stated assumptions; it is not a detection rate.

Ray tracing

A model of how a radio path may travel through terrain or around/among buildings. It can identify plausible obstructions and paths; it does not replace site measurement.
