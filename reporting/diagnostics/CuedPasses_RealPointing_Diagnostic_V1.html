Cued passes with the real antenna pointing: still no detection

On 29 September the antenna pointing was established on site: until that day SURV had been pointing about 060° true (north-east), not 270° (west) as the watcher and the earlier tracking reports assumed. That evening five cued passes were captured with the pointing known (SURV about 304°, REF about 090°, all LNAs removed, gain [10 0]). Two passes fired on stale track states that only appeared to be in the beam; the watcher now ignores states older than 30 s. The other three were genuinely in the beam, two with good ADS-B truth, and none produced a detection near the cued aircraft. Re-examined against SURV's real pointing, only 4 of the 39 cued captures of 27–28 September had the aircraft in the beam, so those runs say little about sensitivity.

          Stale alerts: the alert's bearing against a fresh fix

          Each alert prints the bearing of the track state it used. Comparing it with the nearest fresh fix shows whether that state was current. On 29 September two alerts were off by 24° and 30° with fixes 3–5 minutes old. On 27–28 September the differences are explained by the aircraft's own motion between the alert and the next fresh fix (close aircraft turn through bearing quickly); the largest unexplained lag is about 10 s, inside the new 30 s limit.
