Calibration loop closed from outdoors; the N320's own transmitter only leaks

On 29 September a vertical monopole on the N320's RF0 TX/RX port was tried as a built-in calibration source. It does radiate (an independent receiver hears it 75 ft away), but the only place the N320 sees it is SURV, through leakage inside the radio: the level is the same with SURV's input on a 50 Ω load. Neither Yagi hears it over the air. The Pluto, moved outdoors to the corner of the stairwell with a horizontal antenna, closes the loop on both channels: +39.7 dB on SURV (turned to face it) and +12.4 dB on REF (side-on), with SURV silent when loaded. A gain sweep with every LNA removed shows the chain is linear only up to N320 gain 10 on SURV and 15 on REF; above that the front end compresses. The N320 has no automatic gain control.

Loop closed on both channels from an independent source: SURV +39.7 dB (z 67.5), REF +12.4 dB (z 20.8), repeatable to 0.4 dB · null check passed (SURV on 50 Ω: nothing)
The N320's own transmitter is not a usable calibration source as installed: SURV's +21 dB is internal TX/RX → RX2 leakage, and REF hears nothing over the air at TX gain 30, −20 dBFS

          Why not AGC?

          • The N320 has none. Its UHD property tree exposes one control per receive front end, rx_frontends/0/gains/all, and no AGC node. Hardware AGC exists on AD9361-based radios (Pluto, E320, B2xx), not on the N320.
          • A running AGC would hurt this radar even where it exists: a gain step inside a coherent processing interval breaks the cross-ambiguity integration and the ECA clutter cancellation, and it changes the REF/SURV ratio this calibration measures.
          • What works instead is set-and-hold: before each capture, step the gain and watch total output power. It rises 1 dB per dB up to the knee and then flattens (table above), so the knee can be found without the Pluto. Hold the gain one or two steps below it for the whole capture.
