          What moving the injector buys

          The 602.05 MHz loop test measured the coupling directly; the free-space table shows how much of it is distance.

          • • Distance alone gives only 3–8 dB. Going from the stairwell to the long-leg midpoint shortens the REF path by 3.1 dB and the SURV path by 7.9 dB.
          • • Measured coupling (602.05 MHz, Pluto DAC → N320): −56.8 dB to REF, −74.2 dB to SURV. Free-space loss differs by only 5 dB between the two paths, so most of SURV's extra 12 dB loss most likely comes from its antenna pattern (the stairwell is further off its main lobe) and the glass.
          • • SURV needs about 20 dB more for per-bearing calibration. A position outside the stairwell and in front of the SURV Yagi addresses both pattern and glass. Glass losses (coated or low-E: 10–30+ dB at UHF) and side-lobe levels (10–25 dB below boresight) are literature estimates, not measurements here.
          • • Watch the placement between the antennas. On the REF–SURV line the injector is broadside to both Yagis if they point roughly perpendicular to that leg, and end-on to them if the leg runs along their pointing direction. Placing it 10–20 ft in front of the midpoint, toward where both Yagis point, keeps it in both main lobes.
          • • The REF is now a Yagi, not an omni. The azimuth scan assumes a stable reference. With a directional REF, the calibration comb's REF level also depends on the injector bearing.
