Pluto calibration signal: is it reaching the N320?

The N320 receive chain has been overloaded at the gain used for every capture so far. The REF path has two LNAs in series (the antenna's own and a second wideband LNA ahead of 100 ft of coax), and the local DTV transmitters 9–11 km away arrive at about −14 dBm per channel. At N320 gain [SURV 30, REF 50] dB neither channel follows a gain change; REF is overloaded even at gain 0. At gain [10 0] a single Pluto carrier 50 kHz above each of 21 channel boundaries from 476 to 602 MHz is detected on REF at all of them and on SURV at 20. SURV sees it up to 31 dB above its floor in each 0.1 s frame (536–548 MHz), which is likely enough for a frame-averaged phase calibration without moving the injector. A new tab maps all 24 DTV emitters in the site's transmitter table, with what the N320 sees from each. The 12-tone comb was never seen because, split twelve ways, each tone fell just under the detection threshold.

          What moving the injector buys

          The channel-boundary loop tests measured the coupling directly; the free-space table shows how much of it is distance.

          • • Distance alone gives only 3–8 dB. Going from the stairwell to the long-leg midpoint shortens the REF path by 3.1 dB and the SURV path by 7.9 dB.
          • • At equal N320 gain SURV receives the stairwell carrier 22–49 dB below REF at 20 of 21 boundaries from 476 to 602 MHz (7.5 dB at 524 MHz). Free-space loss differs by only 5 dB between the two paths, so most of the difference most likely comes from SURV's antenna pattern (the stairwell is about 145° off its boresight, on its back side), the glass, and REF's extra LNA. The 17 dB measured on 2026-09-23 was taken with REF overloaded.
          • • SURV's margin is now up to 31 dB per 0.1 s frame at N320 gain 10 (536–548 MHz). That is likely enough for a frame-averaged phase calibration; if more is needed, a position outside the stairwell and in front of the SURV Yagi addresses both pattern and glass. Glass losses (coated or low-E: 10–30+ dB at UHF) and side-lobe levels (10–25 dB below boresight) are literature estimates, not measurements here.
          • • Watch the placement between the antennas. On the REF–SURV line the injector is broadside to both Yagis if they point roughly perpendicular to that leg, and end-on to them if the leg runs along their pointing direction. Placing it 10–20 ft in front of the midpoint, toward where both Yagis point, keeps it in both main lobes.
          • • The REF is now a Yagi, not an omni. The azimuth scan assumes a stable reference. With a directional REF, the calibration comb's REF level also depends on the injector bearing.
