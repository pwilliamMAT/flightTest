System Engineering: Design Process and Status

Passive bistatic radar testbed — formal system design process, separate from the exploration report family

Status snapshot: 26 September 2026. The masters of everything summarised here are in flightTest docs/system/; where a page and a master disagree, the master wins.

Purpose of this section

Bottom line (ELI5): The exploration reports ask “can we do it, and what does it look like?”. This section asks a different question: what exactly is the system we are building, what must it do, how is it put together, and how do we know each part works? It follows a formal design process, from the mission down to the individual parts, and records what has been checked so far.

The project has two mission goals: to collect passive radar data showing that a complete system can be built with MATLAB and MathWorks tools (MG-1), and to collect data that proves MATLAB functions such as radar, tracking and signal processing work as designed on real data that can be shared with customers (MG-2). The pages below trace those goals through needs and requirements to the architecture, the interfaces, verification evidence, and the system as built. The exploration family in the reporting home keeps its own nine reports and claim boundaries; this section links to them where their evidence is used.

Claim boundary: this section describes the designed system and the verification status of its requirements and interfaces. A requirement or interface marked Verified means that stated check passed on stated evidence; it is not a radar detection, tracking, or localization result. The requirements baseline is a DRAFT for owner review.

Decisions recorded on 26 September 2026

  • Two report tracks. The exploration report family ("can we do it, and what does it look like") stays at nine reports. System engineering is presented in this separate section as a formal design process.
  • Requirements. The baseline is published labelled DRAFT; CR-10 stays open. Values still marked TBD stay TBD for now.
  • Time source. Internet NTP is an accepted time source, with an accepted host-to-host tolerance of 0.1 s (DR-TIME-1). GPS/PPS lock is a hardware troubleshooting to-do, not a blocker for NTP-based timing. The re-check of earlier ADS-B timing stays open.
  • MATLAB exception. The Python Cue Tasker is an accepted named exception under MG-1 (DR-DEP-4).
  • Truth separation. CR-9 is accepted, amended for sites that host several emitters and for captures widened (for example to 12 MHz) to take in adjacent channels: the cued/uncued label covers the whole capture, every product records its emitter, and products on other emitters of a cued collection are still conditional on the cue (page 6). The ICD and architecture follow-up is action A-7.
  • Report 02 criteria. The Report 02 acceptance tests stay in Report 02 and do not become derived requirements.
  • Owners. System, RF and integration actions go to Leif; everything else to Pat.
  • Code location. CueListener and the dtv* scripts stay on feature/adsb-cue-listener, with a review action for Pat (A-1).
  • Release build. No full TechnicalSummaryFamily release build is run: it needs files that exist only on the Windows workstation. This is recorded as a known limitation.
